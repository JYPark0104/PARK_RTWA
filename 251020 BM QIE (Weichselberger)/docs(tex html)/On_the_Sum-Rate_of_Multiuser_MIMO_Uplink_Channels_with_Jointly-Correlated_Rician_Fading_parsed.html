On the Sum-Rate of Multiuser MIMO Uplink
Channels with Jointly-Correlated Rician Fading


Chao-Kai Wen, Member, IEEE, Shi Jin, Member, IEEE, and Kai-Kit Wong, Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract-In this paper, we study the capacity-achieving input
covariance matrices for the multiuser multiple-input multiple-
output (MIMO) uplink channel under jointly-correlated Rician
fading when perfect channel state information (CSI) is known at
the receiver, or CSIR while only statistical CSI at the transmitter,
or CSIT, is available. The jointly-correlated MIMO channel (or
the Weichselberger model) accounts for the correlation at two
link ends and is shown to be highly accurate to model real
channels. Classically, numerical techniques together with Monte-
Carlo methods (named stochastic programming) are used to
resolve the problem concerned but at a high computational cost.
To tackle this, we derive the asymptotic sum-rate of the multiuser
(MU) MIMO uplink channel in the large-system regime where
the numbers of antennas at the transmitters and the receiver go
to infinity with constant ratios. Several insights are gained from
the analytic asymptotic sum-rate expression, based on which an
efficient optimization algorithm is further proposed to obtain the
capacity-achieving input covariance matrices. Simulation results
demonstrate that even for a moderate number of antennas at each
link, the new approach provides indistinguishable results as those
obtained by the complex stochastic programming approach.


Index Terms-Asymptotic analysis, ergodic capacity, MIMO,
replica method, jointly-correlated MIMO channel, Weichsel-

The problem at hand is subject to the knowledge on the
channel state, commonly known as channel state information
(CSI), at the receiver and transmitters. Typically, accurate CSI
at the receiver (CSIR) is available through periodic training at
the receiver and is usually assumed in most studies. A stronger
assumption is that perfect CSIR is fed back via a dedicated
noiseless delay-free channel to the transmitter to have perfect
CSI at the transmitter (CSIT). However, this has much less
practical interest, as CSIT is destined to be imperfect due to
factors such as Doppler, feedback delay, quantization errors
and so on. In order to devise transceiver designs that are robust
to errors in CSIT, statistical schemes [4]-[11] emerged as an
effective means and can guarantee certain system performance
averaged over the channel realizations.


To model statistical CSIT, a realistic and analytically
tractable MIMO channel model is required and the Kronecker
(or the separately-correlated) model [3] is one such model
that has been widely adopted. However, the Kronecker model
has been shown to be insufficient to represent the varieties
of practical channels [20] and several more advanced models
have been proposed, including the virtual channel model
[12], [21], the unitary-independent-unitary (UIU) model [22,
Section 3.3.4], and the Weichselberger model [23]. In contrast
to the Kronecker model, the Weichselberger model does
not only account for the correlation at both ends but also
models their mutual dependence. As such, the Weichselberger
model provides a better construction than any other models in
representing a variety of channel scenarios [24]. Throughout
this paper, we shall carefully name a specific channel model

Nevertheless, the results were limited to Kronecker Rayleigh
fading MIMO channels. For SU Kronecker Rayleigh fading
MIMO channels, it was shown in [5], [6] that the capacity-
achieving input covariance matrix aligns with the eigenvectors
of the transmitter-side correlation matrix. This result also
has been verified for the UIU spatial correlation model [22,
Section 3.3.4] and the virtual channel representation [12].
If LOS is present, then this result holds only for the SU
uncorrelated Rician fading MIMO channel [13]. Even more
challenging would be the optimization of the input covariance
matrices for MU-MIMO channels. In [14], it was shown that
the eigenvectors of the capacity-achieving input covariance
matrix of each user align with those of its own transmitter-
side correlation matrix when the MU Kronecker Rayleigh
fading MIMO channel is considered. The eigenvalues of the
capacity-achieving input covariance matrices were obtained
by numerical techniques in [15]. Being the most general
model, results for the Weichselberger model are, however,
rare. The only known results so far appeared in [17] which
considered the capacity-achieving input covariance matrix for
an SU Weichselberger Rician fading MIMO channel and more
recently in [18] which characterized the problem for the MU
cases only in the low-power regime.


Except for a few special cases, getting explicit eigenvector
expressions for the capacity-achieving input covariance matri-
ces for general channels seems hopeless. Thus, most works
addressing the input covariance matrices for the general cases
resorted to numerical techniques (e.g., [8]), and in fact all the
numerical techniques in [8], [15], [22] belong to stochastic
programming [25], which is an optimization programming
that incorporates random elements in the objective function,
the constraints, or both. The Monte-Carlo method is a way
to generate samples of random variables, from which related
parameters are then computed. This method implies unfortu-
nately a huge amount of computations and is prohibitively
time-consuming.


The main obstacle of the problem concerned lies in the
derivation of the average sum-rate of the MU Weichselberger
Rician fading MIMO channel. A prominent approach to cir-
cumvent the difficulty is the use of large-system analysis,
in which the derivation is done under the condition that the
numbers of transmit and receive antennas go to infinity at
a fixed rate. Remarkably, the asymptotic results have been
able to provide highly accurate estimates even with small
numbers of antennas. For example, Dumont et al. in [26]
proposed an asymptotic approximation for the average mutual
information of an SU Kronecker Rician fading MIMO channel
and also developed an efficient way to compute the capacity-
achieving input covariance matrix. The algorithm in [26] not
only provides a computationally attractive approach, but also
indistinguishable average mutual information results compared
to those obtained by stochastic programming, e.g., [8]. Related
results were extended from the SU case to the MU scenarios
in [27] for Kronecker MIMO channels such as the multipath
cases [28] and the multiuser cases [29] as special cases.


In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic (or large-
system) sum-rate of the MU Weichselberger Rician fading
MIMO channel in the uplink. Our contributions are summa-
rized as follows:


· We derive the asymptotic sum-rate of the MU Weichsel-
berger Rician fading MIMO channel in the large-system
regime. Simulation results reveal that even for systems
with only a few antennas at each terminal, the asymptotic
expression provides reliable estimates to those obtained
by Monte-Carlo simulations for a wide range of signal-
to-noise ratio (SNR).
· An asymptotic programming approach, which can be per-
formed by an SU optimization over a deterministic equiv-
alent channel, is then proposed to efficiently compute
the asymptotic-optimal input covariance matrices for the
MU channel. Simulation results show a perfect agreement
between results obtained by asymptotic and stochastic
programming, while asymptotic programming is compu-
tationally much more efficient because no Monte-Carlo
procedure is required.
· Also, we provide a few useful observations on the struc-
tures of the asymptotic-optimal input covariance matrices
when statistical CSIT is available.


Our analysis is based on a powerful mathematical tool from
statistical physics, known as the replica method, which was
originally developed in statistical physics to study spin glasses
[30] and has since been widely used in a variety of information
processing problems, see [31]. The replica method was first
applied to study the asymptotic mutual information of code-
division multiple-access (CDMA) channels with antipodal
inputs by Tanaka [32]. Inspired by Tanaka's work, there have
been many recent results successfully applying the replica
method in communications systems [33], [34], [36]-[40].

VI. CONCLUSION


In this paper, we derived the asymptotic sum-rate for the
MU Weichselberger (jointly-correlated) Rician MIMO channel
in the large-system limit. Results showed that the asymptotic
result is accurate even with a moderate numbers of antennas.
Based on this result, we further developed an efficient algo-
rithm to find the asymptotic-optimal input covariance matrices
that can maximize the asymptotic sum-rate if statistical CSIT
is exploited. The proposed asymptotic programming approach
has been demonstrated to provide indistinguishable results as
those obtained by a stochastic (Monte-Carlo) programming
approach.


Finally, note that the asymptotic sum-rate expression was
derived based on the replica method, which strictly speaking,
is not mathematically rigorous, even though the asymptotic
sum-rate expression has been confirmed by Monte Carlo
simulations. Future advances in random matrix theory such
as in [29], [44] will be required to establish the mathematical
rigor and the limiting behavior of Proposition 1. In addition,
a mathematical justification on the convergence of Algorithm
1 is still an open problem.
